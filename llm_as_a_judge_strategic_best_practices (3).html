Frontier LLM / LLM Scale
S t r a t e g y
Utilize frontier LLMs – GPT-4/5, Claude Opus/Sonnet, Gemini pro/Flash class models.

Judge should usually be at least as capable as the generator (preferably stronger).
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Judging requires evidence-sensitive reasoning: claim extraction, source comparison, unsupported-inference detection, and aggregation. Current research supports the usage of frontier models for high human agreement rates.

G-Eval p=0.514; MT-Bench >80% human agreement [Liu et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023].
Smaller LLMs
S t r a t e g y
Smaller LLMs can be utilized under some conditions: Use when fine-tuned (narrow task; sufficiently specified), with a jury/panel setup.

Fine-tuned LLM judges cannot be generalized (task-specific; not general-purpose hallucination detectors, Huang et al., 2025).
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Fine-tuning and aggregation reduce judge burden, improves judge accuracy and improve cost/throughput. Research shows comparable judge accuracy as GPT-4:
• Prometheus 13B Pearson=0.897 vs humans;
• PandaLM-7B: 93.75% of GPT-3.5 and 88.28% of GPT-4;
• JudgeLM-7B judges 5K samples in ~3 minutes;
• PoLL (Haiku, Command R, GPT-3.5) >7x cheaper and outperforms GPT-4.
Cross-Model Judging
S t r a t e g y
Use a judge from a different model family than the generator, but judge must still be capable.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
reduces self-preference/familiarity bias, but judge must still be capable. Self-preference bias observed with internal experimentation, GPT-4 (Wataoka et al., 2024); G-Eval notes bias toward LLM-generated texts (Liu et al., 2023).

Define Task + Metric
S t r a t e g y
State role, task, metric, and failure condition explicitly.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
LLM judges infer the objective from natural-language instructions; ambiguity increases prompt sensitivity. LLM-as-Judge reliability requires prompt standardization (Gu et al., 2024). RAGAS shows that separating evaluation into clearly defined dimensions is necessary.
Chain of Thought Reasoning
S t r a t e g y
Judges perform more consistently when the evaluation is broken into explicit reasoning steps before the final verdict is produced.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Chain-of-thought prompting improves performance on complex reasoning tasks... leading to more consistent and accurate judgments (Wei et al., 2022). CoT makes the decision process more traceable and verdicts more accurate.
Schemas / Style
S t r a t e g y
Require JSON fields for claims/facts, evidence, reasoning, and verdict.

Tell the judge to ignore fluency, polish, and length unless they are target metrics.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Structured schema reduces formatting variance, simplifies parsing, improves auditability.
LLM judges can over-reward longer or more polished answers even when correctness is weak. Position, verbosity, and stylistic preferences are known reliability concerns.
Task Decomposition
S t r a t e g y
Decompose evaluation into stages → Claim/fact extraction → evidence verification → aggregation → verdict.

Require minimal source evidence for supported/contradicted claims.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Complex judgments become more reliable when split into simpler sub-tasks (Gu et al., 2024). LLMs perform better on multi-step reasoning tasks when explicitly specified (CoT). Claim-evidence attribution makes verdicts traceable.
Few-Shot Examples
S t r a t e g y
Include examples of judgements for correct and incorrect model outputs.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Anchors task interpretation, clarifies edge cases. LLMs exhibit strong few-shot generalization capabilities; performance increases with in-context demonstrations [Brown et al., 2020].

Aggregation Across Judges
S t r a t e g y
Heterogeneous judge panel → Combine judges from different model families. Reduces dependence on one model's bias profile; similar judges may share correlated errors.

Approaches: Majority voting, average score aggregation, reliability weighted aggregation.
Usage scenarios: Impracticality of frontier LLMs, high-risk evaluations when annotations are constrained, if "which judge is best" depends on the input.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
PoLL shows diverse panels (Haiku, Command R, GPT-3.5) can outperform a single large judge and reduce intra-model bias [Verga et al., 2024].

Reliability weighted aggregation: not all judges are equally reliable; human/gold calibration can assign greater influence to stronger judges. David-Skene and GLAD provide statistical intuition [Dawid & Skene 1979; Whitehill 2009].
Reporting Scores
S t r a t e g y
Ratio-based scores:
Faithfulness = supported claims / total claims
Completeness = covered facts / required facts
Why: Captures partial correctness and provides transparent evidence-grounded scores.

Rubric-based scores: Use 1-5 scales when partial quality levels matter. Why: Makes scoring thresholds explicit.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
Continuous scores: RAGAS-style source-grounded scoring [Es et al., 2023].

Rubric approach makes scoring reliable and interpretable [Gu et al., 2024].
Judge Evaluation
S t r a t e g y
Report failure capture: Use hallucination capture rate for faithfulness and incompleteness capture rate for completeness. Why: False negatives are often more consequential than false alarms.

Use continuous metrics: Accuracy, precision, F1, Cohen's kappa, and Spearman correlation (for continuous scores). Why: Captures overall agreement, false-alarm tradeoffs.
S u p p o r t i n g   R e s e a r c h
MT-Bench, Vectara uses human preference agreement; G-Eval uses human-score correlation [Zheng et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023].

RAGTruth, HaluEval, FaithDial, Faithfulness/HHEM-style evaluations emphasize hallucination/failure rates.

G-Eval → Spearman correlation [Liu et al., 2023].
